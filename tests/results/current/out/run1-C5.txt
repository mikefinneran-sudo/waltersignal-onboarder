A good research brief is one page, written before the call, and built only from public sources. The template and a filled example are in `reference/templates.md`.

**The template**

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Business | Name, town, what they do, how many people |
| Fit | Why it fits (`reference/clients.md`), or why it may not |
| Who decides | The owner-operator's name, if public |
| Likely champion | The office manager or equivalent, if public |
| What they use now | Tools visible on their website or job posts |
| Why-now signals | New location, hiring, reviews, a season coming |
| Three questions | The first three you will ask, from `reference/discovery-questions.md` |
| Sources | Every link you used |

**What the example shows** (Example Heating & Air, a made-up practice business)

- **Fit** gives a reason, not just "good": growing, hiring, and the job post asks for spreadsheet skills.
- **Names** come from public pages: the owner from the About page, the likely champion from a job post for a second dispatcher.
- **Tools** come from what is visible: an online booking widget on the site, and "spreadsheet skills" in a job post.
- **Why now** is specific: hiring a second dispatcher, and heating season starting.
- **The three questions** are picked by number from the question bank (#4, #6, #12), each matched to what the research turned up.
- **Sources** are listed: company website, job post, Google Business Profile.

**Where it goes:** save each brief in the client's folder in the Vault (`reference/second-brain.md`), under its CRM stage.
