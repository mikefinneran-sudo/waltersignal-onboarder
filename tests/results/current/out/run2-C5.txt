A good research brief is one page, written before the call, with all eight fields filled in from public sources and every link listed. The template and a worked example are in `reference/templates.md`.

**The eight fields**

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Business | Name, town, what they do, how many people |
| Fit | Why it fits (judged against `reference/clients.md`), or why it may not |
| Who decides | The owner-operator's name, if public |
| Likely champion | The office manager or equivalent, if public |
| What they use now | Tools visible on their website or job posts |
| Why-now signals | New location, hiring, reviews, a season coming |
| Three questions | The first three you will ask, from `reference/discovery-questions.md` |
| Sources | Every link you used |

**The practice example in the template**

```
Business:            Example Heating & Air (practice only), Springfield, residential HVAC, ~25 people
Fit:                 Good. Growing, hiring, and the job post asks for spreadsheet skills.
Who decides:         The owner, named on the About page
Likely champion:     Office manager, named in a job post for a second dispatcher
What they use now:   Online booking widget on the site; job post asks for "spreadsheet skills"
Why-now signals:     Hiring a second dispatcher; heating season starting
Three questions:     #4 (call to job), #6 (falls through the cracks), #12 (why this quarter)
Sources:             company website, job post, Google Business Profile
```

**What the example does well**
- Every claim is tied to something visible, such as a job post or the About page.
- The fit line gives the reason, not just "good."
- The why-now signals are specific and current.
- The questions are picked by number from the bank, and they match what the research found.
- The sources are listed, so someone else can check them.

**Where it goes:** save the brief in the client's folder in the Vault (`reference/second-brain.md`), under the lead's CRM stage.
