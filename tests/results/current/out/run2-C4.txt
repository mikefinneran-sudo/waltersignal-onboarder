The full question bank is in `reference/discovery-questions.md`. Pick two or three per section; a discovery call is a conversation, not a survey. The call runs in this order (`reference/sales-process.md`): current state, gap, solution, next step.

**Opening: why are we talking?**
1. "What made you reach out now?"
2. "If one part of the business ran better a year from now, which part would it be?"
3. "How did you hear about us?" (Log the source in the CRM: referral, website, event, or outreach.)

**Current state: how does the work run today?**

4. "Walk me through what happens from the moment a customer calls you."
5. "What gets written on paper, or kept in someone's head?"
6. "Where does a job fall through the cracks?"
7. "Which tools do you pay for today, and which ones does anyone actually open?"
8. "Who in the office lives with this every day?" (That is usually the champion.)

**Gap: what does it cost?**

9. "How often does that happen in a month?"
10. "What does one of those cost you, in time or in a lost job?"
11. "If nothing changes, what does this look like a year from now?"

**Why now, why us**

12. "What changed that makes this worth fixing this quarter?"
13. "Have you tried to fix it before? What happened?"
14. "What would you need to see to trust a fix?"

**Money and decision** (money comes up by the second conversation at the latest)

15. "Who besides you weighs in on a decision like this?"
16. "Do you have a range in mind, or would it help to see options?"
17. "When would you want this working?"

**Next step (every call)**

18. "Here's what I'll do next, by [date]. Here's what I need from you. Does that work?"

Write the next step in the CRM the same day: a date, an owner, and what happens.

**Never ask**
- "Do you want to use AI?" Lead with their work, not the technology.
- "What's your budget?" in the first five minutes. Earn it with the gap questions first.
- Anything about competitors' private pricing or the prospect's personal details.
