Here is the Brennan Mechanical story, from `reference/case-study.md`, with the pieces you need to tell it in two minutes.

## The story in five beats

| Beat | What to say |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A 38-person heating, cooling, and plumbing contractor serving three counties. The owner was the decider and the office manager was the champion. |
| **Problem** | Quotes took two days because an estimator rebuilt each one from a spreadsheet price book and old jobs. Dispatch ran on a whiteboard. Every finished job was typed again into the accounting software. |
| **Discovery Sprint (2 weeks)** | We interviewed the owner, office manager, two estimators, and a dispatcher. We ranked quoting first and dispatch second, and left the accounting software alone. |
| **Pilot (7 weeks)** | A quote builder pulls the price book and past jobs and drafts each quote with an AI step. An estimator approves every quote before it goes out. The number we agreed to measure was time from request to quote sent. |
| **Build (5 months)** | A dispatch board, a technician phone app for job notes and photos, and an automatic hand-off of finished jobs to the accounting software. Staff were trained by role. |

**Result (Brennan's numbers):** median time to send a quote fell from two business days to four hours during the Pilot. After the Build, finished jobs stopped being typed twice. Brennan now pays for the Standard Support Plan.

## A two-minute version to practice

> Brennan Mechanical is a 38-person heating, cooling, and plumbing contractor. Quotes took them two days, because an estimator rebuilt each one by hand from a spreadsheet price book and old jobs. Dispatch lived on a whiteboard, and every finished job got typed again into the accounting software.
>
> We started with a two-week Discovery Sprint. We talked to the owner, the office manager, two estimators, and a dispatcher. We ranked quoting first, dispatch second, and told them to leave the accounting software alone.
>
> Then a seven-week Pilot on quoting only. The system drafts each quote from their price book and past jobs, and an estimator approves every one before it goes out. We agreed on one number up front: time from request to quote sent. The median went from two business days to four hours.
>
> Because they saw that on their own numbers, they went ahead with the Build: a dispatch board, a technician phone app, and an automatic hand-off to accounting. Finished jobs are no longer typed twice.

## What to point out

- **The Sprint recommended less where less was right.** Telling Brennan to leave the accounting software alone saved them money. This answers "AI is all hype" (`reference/objections.md`).
- **Each phase stood alone.** A client can stop after any phase and keep what was delivered (`reference/services.md`).
- **A person approved every AI draft.** That is true of every engagement.

## Rules for telling it

- **Brennan's numbers are Brennan's.** Tell the four-hour result as their result. Never promise a prospect the same number (`reference/the-message.md`).
- **Leave out the prices.** The case study lists what Brennan paid, but you quote no price to anyone before the Launch Gate on day 10. The Build price is Internal, so it is never said to a client, before or after launch (`reference/services.md`).
- **Sending anything goes through the Head of Sales.** If you put this story in writing for a prospect, they approve it first.
- **Practice it with the Head of Sales.** Telling a case study in two minutes is part of the module 6 milestone (`reference/syllabus.md`). Only the Head of Sales signs it off.

On day 10 you co-run a live call with the Head of Sales on the line.
