The decider signs and approves the money. The champion is the person you win over first. Both are in `reference/clients.md`.

| Role at the prospect | Who it usually is | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decider** | Owner, CEO, or general manager | Signs the agreement and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| **Champion** | Usually the operations manager or office manager | Lives with the problem every day and knows where work falls through the cracks | Win them first. They tell you how the work really runs, and the decider listens to them |
| **Users** | Dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery. Their answers make the Discovery Sprint sharper |

Two examples from `reference/case-study.md`:
- **Brennan Mechanical:** the owner was the decider and the office manager was the champion.
- **Harbor Dental Group:** the operations director was the champion, and the two dentist-owners were the deciders. A deal can have more than one decider.

Two questions in `reference/discovery-questions.md` find these people on a call:
- **#8:** "Who in the office lives with this every day?" This is usually the champion.
- **#15:** "Who besides you weighs in on a decision like this?" This surfaces other deciders.

For research briefs, the template in `reference/templates.md` has a "Who decides" line and a "Likely champion" line. Fill them in from public sources, such as the About page or a job post.
