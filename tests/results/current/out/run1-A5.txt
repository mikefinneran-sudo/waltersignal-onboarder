The decider is who signs and approves the money. The champion is who you win over first (`reference/clients.md`).

| Role | Who it usually is | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| **Decider** | The owner, CEO, or general manager | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| **Champion** | Usually the operations manager or office manager | Win them first. They live with the problem daily and know where work falls through the cracks, and the decider listens to them |
| **Users** | Dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads | Ask to talk to one during discovery. Their answers sharpen the Discovery Sprint |

The case studies show the pattern (`reference/case-study.md`):
- **Brennan Mechanical:** the owner is the decider and the office manager is the champion.
- **Harbor Dental Group:** the operations director is the champion and the two dentist-owners are the deciders. Deciders can be more than one person.

For your day-4 research brief, `reference/templates.md` has a "Who decides" line (the owner-operator's name, if public) and a "Likely champion" line (the office manager or equivalent, if public). In discovery, question 8 finds the champion ("Who in the office lives with this every day?") and question 15 finds other voices in the decision (`reference/discovery-questions.md`).
