Say this (`reference/objections.md`):

> "A lot of it is. That's why we start by mapping how your work actually runs, and we'll tell you where AI doesn't belong. On one client's Discovery Sprint, we told them to leave their accounting software alone."

That last line is the Brennan Mechanical story (`reference/case-study.md`). Underneath this objection, the prospect has usually been sold to and burned, so agreeing with them first works better than defending AI.

Then move off the technology and back to their work with a current-state question (`reference/discovery-questions.md`):

- "Walk me through what happens from the moment a customer calls you."
- "Where does a job fall through the cracks?"
- "Which tools do you pay for today, and which ones does anyone actually open?"

Two more points from the folder can help if they push:

- **AI steps always have a person approving.** Anything AI drafts is checked by the client's staff before it goes out (`reference/services.md`).
- **Do not promise them a result.** If you tell Brennan's story, tell it as Brennan's result with Brennan's numbers (`reference/the-message.md`).

Also avoid leading with "AI agents," "LLMs," or tool names. Lead with their work.
