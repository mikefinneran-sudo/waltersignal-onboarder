Agree with them, then show you will not push AI where it does not belong. This is from `reference/objections.md`.

**Say:**
> "A lot of it is. That's why we start by mapping how your work runs, and we'll tell you where AI doesn't belong. Brennan Mechanical's Sprint said to leave their accounting software alone."

**Then ask a current-state question** from `reference/discovery-questions.md` to bring the conversation back to their work:
- #4: "Walk me through what happens from the moment a customer calls you."
- #6: "Where does a job fall through the cracks?"
- #7: "Which tools do you pay for today, and which ones does anyone actually open?"

**Why it works:** they have likely been sold to and burned. You are agreeing with them, not defending AI.

**Supporting facts you can use** (all true of every engagement):
- Brennan's Sprint recommended leaving their accounting software alone, and that saved them money (`reference/case-study.md`).
- AI steps always have a person approving. Anything AI drafts is checked by the client's staff before it goes out (`reference/services.md`).
- "If AI is not the right answer for a step, we will tell you" is on the Say This list (`reference/the-message.md`).

**Avoid:**
- Do not lead with the technology ("AI agents," "LLMs," tool names). Lead with their work.
- If you tell the Brennan story, give it as Brennan's result. Never promise this prospect the same number.
