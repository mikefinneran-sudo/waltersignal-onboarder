NEW HIRE: OK. What's our pitch?
ONBOARDER: Here is the 30-second pitch (`reference/the-message.md`):

> WalterSignal builds custom software and AI automation for established businesses whose work has outgrown spreadsheets and disconnected tools. We start by mapping how your work actually runs, prove one fix working on your own numbers, and only then build the rest. Everything we build, you own.

Say it in your own words once you know it, but every version must keep all four parts:

| Part | What it says | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Who it is for** | Established businesses of roughly 10 to 250 people, running on spreadsheets, whiteboards, and tools that don't talk to each other | They recognize themselves, or they aren't a fit |
| **Understand first** | We map how the work runs before recommending anything | They've been pitched tools by people who never asked how they work |
| **Prove before building** | One workflow goes live and is measured before the full build | They never fund a large project on the strength of a slide |
| **You own it** | The client owns the code, the data, and the accounts | No lock-in, no per-user fees, no subscription to us |

**Never say this.** Your module 1 sign-off asks you to name three of these (`reference/syllabus.md`):

- Never promise a delivery date. Dates come from Solutions Engineering after scoping.
- Never promise a result (hours saved, revenue gained) for a prospect's business. Results in `reference/case-study.md` belong to those clients and are told as theirs.
- Never discount, or hint at one. Discounts belong to the Founder & CEO.
- Never lead with the technology ("AI agents," "LLMs," tool names). Lead with the client's work.
- Never claim WalterSignal sells a product or a software subscription. It does client work only.

**Lines that work:**
- "Before we recommend anything, we want to see how the work actually runs."
- "You'll see one piece working on your own numbers before you commit to the rest."
- "You own what we build: the code, the data, and the accounts are in your name."
- "If AI is not the right answer for a step, we will tell you."

**Module 1 is signed off** by the Head of Sales when you can say the pitch from memory with all four parts and name three things never to say. Say it aloud at the 4:00 PM Eastern check-in.

### TURN 3
NEW HIRE: Got it. Who are our clients?
ONBOARDER: Our clients are established businesses of roughly 10 to 250 employees, with revenue coming in and a real operation to improve. They are not startups still finding customers (`reference/clients.md`).

**What makes a good fit**
- **Situation:** the work runs on spreadsheets, whiteboards, email, and paper, or on several tools that don't share information, so people type the same thing twice.
- **What you'll hear:** "Quotes take us two days." "Only Sarah knows how that works." "We re-enter every order into the accounting system." "I can't see where jobs stand without calling around."
- **Common industries:** field service (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), distribution and wholesale, light manufacturing, multi-location healthcare offices (dental, physical therapy), and professional services firms. No industry is excluded; judge each business on its own facts.

**Who you talk to inside them**

| Role | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| **Decider:** owner, CEO, or general manager | Signs the agreement and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| **Champion:** usually the operations or office manager | Lives with the problem daily and knows where work falls through the cracks | Win them first. They tell you how the work really runs, and the decider listens to them |
| **Users:** dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery |

**Not a fit.** Say so early and kindly, and log the reason in the CRM.

| They want | What to say |
|---|---|
| A website or marketing | "That isn't work we do. We build the software that runs the operation behind the website." |
| IT support, computers, or networks | "That's an IT provider's job, not ours." |
| A developer by the hour | "We work in fixed phases so you know the cost before we start." |
| A problem too small for a $20,000 Pilot | Log it as Closed Lost with the reason "too small" |

**The four qualifying questions.** Ask all four before spending real time on a lead:
1. **Why this?** What problem, in their words?
2. **Why now?** What changed that makes it worth fixing this quarter?
3. **Why us?** What would make WalterSignal the right fit over doing nothing or buying a tool?
4. **Is it big enough?** Would fixing it be worth more to them than a $20,000 Pilot?

If a lead can't answer "Why now?", log it and set a follow-up date. Don't chase it.

**Module 2 sign-off** (`reference/syllabus.md`): describe a good-fit client, the decider, the champion, and the four questions, then sort five sample businesses into fit and not a fit. The Head of Sales gives you the five on day 2.

Real client stories come with module 3 on day 2 (`reference/case-study.md`).
