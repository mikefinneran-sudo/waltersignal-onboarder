### TURN 3
NEW HIRE: Got it. Who are our clients?
ONBOARDER: Our clients are established businesses of roughly 10 to 250 employees, with revenue coming in and a real operation to improve. Not startups still finding customers (`reference/clients.md`).

**A good fit looks like this:**
- The work runs on spreadsheets, whiteboards, email, and paper, or on several tools that do not share information, so people type the same thing twice.
- Phrases you will hear: "Quotes take us two days." "Only Sarah knows how that works." "We re-enter every order into the accounting system." "I can't see where jobs stand without calling around."
- Common industries are field service (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), distribution and wholesale, light manufacturing, multi-location healthcare offices (dental, physical therapy), and professional services firms. No industry is excluded; judge each business on its own facts.

**Who you talk to inside them:**

| Role | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| Decider (owner, CEO, or general manager) | Signs the agreement and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| Champion (usually the operations or office manager) | Lives with the problem daily and knows where work falls through the cracks | Win them first. The decider listens to them |
| Users (dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads) | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery |

**Not a fit.** Say so early and kindly, and log the reason in the CRM.

| They want | Why it is not a fit |
|---|---|
| A website or marketing | WalterSignal does not build marketing websites |
| IT support, computers, or networks | Not client work WalterSignal does |
| A developer by the hour | WalterSignal sells scoped phases, not hours |
| A problem too small for a $20,000 Pilot | The smallest phase after the Sprint is $20,000. Log it as Closed Lost, reason "too small" |

**Four qualifying questions.** Ask all four before spending real time on a lead:
1. **Why this?** What problem, in their words?
2. **Why now?** What changed that makes it worth fixing this quarter?
3. **Why us?** What would make WalterSignal the right fit over doing nothing or buying a tool?
4. **Is it big enough?** Would fixing it be worth more to them than a $20,000 Pilot?

If a lead cannot answer "Why now?", log it and set a follow-up date. Do not chase it.

Module 2 finishes tomorrow, when the Head of Sales gives you five sample businesses to sort into fit and not a fit. The client stories (Brennan Mechanical and Harbor Dental) are module 3, also tomorrow. The $20,000 figure is for your understanding only; you quote no price to anyone until the Launch Gate on day 10.
