ONBOARDER: From `reference/the-message.md`, the 30-second pitch:

> WalterSignal builds custom software and AI automation for established businesses whose work has outgrown spreadsheets and disconnected tools. We start by mapping how your work actually runs, prove one fix working on your own numbers, and only then build the rest. Everything we build, you own.

You can say it in your own words once you know it, but every version must keep all four parts:

| Part | What it says | Why it lands |

| **Understand first** | We map how the work runs before recommending anything | They've been pitched tools by people who never asked how they work |

| **You own it** | The client owns the code, the data, and the accounts | No lock-in, no per-user fees, no subscription to us |

Module 1 is signed off when you can say it from memory with all four parts and name three things never to say. The "Never Say This" list is in the same file. The Head of Sales signs it off at the check-in.

ONBOARDER: From `reference/clients.md`:

**A good-fit client**
- **Size:** an established business of roughly 10 to 250 employees, with revenue coming in and a real operation to improve. Not a startup still finding customers.
- **Situation:** the work runs on spreadsheets, whiteboards, email, and paper, or on several tools that don't share information, so people type the same thing twice.

- **Common industries:** field service (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), distribution and wholesale, light manufacturing, multi-location healthcare offices (dental, physical therapy), and professional services firms. No industry is excluded; judge each business on its own facts.

**Who you talk to inside the client**

| Role | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| **Decider:** owner, CEO, or general manager | Signs the agreement and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they'll be able to see and control |
| **Champion:** usually the operations or office manager | Lives with the problem daily and knows where work falls through the cracks | Win them first. The decider listens to them |
| **Users:** dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery |

The rest of module 2 is also in `clients.md`: what is *not* a fit, and the four qualifying questions (Why this? Why now? Why us? Is it big enough?). Day 2 finishes module 2, when you sort five sample businesses into fit and not a fit.
