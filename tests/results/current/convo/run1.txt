### TURN 3
NEW HIRE: Got it. Who are our clients?
ONBOARDER: From `reference/clients.md`, a good-fit client is:

- **Size:** an established business of roughly 10 to 250 employees, with revenue coming in and a real operation to improve. Not a startup still finding customers.
- **Situation:** the work runs on spreadsheets, whiteboards, email, and paper, or on several tools that don't share information, so people type the same thing twice.
- **What you'll hear:** "Quotes take us two days." "Only Sarah knows how that works." "We re-enter every order into the accounting system."
- **Common industries:** field service (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), distribution and wholesale, light manufacturing, multi-location healthcare offices (dental, physical therapy), and professional services firms. No industry is excluded; judge each business on its own facts.

**Who you talk to inside a client:**

| Role | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| **Decider** (owner, CEO, general manager) | Signs and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| **Champion** (usually the operations or office manager) | Lives with the problem daily | Win them first. They tell you how the work really runs, and the decider listens to them |
| **Users** (dispatchers, estimators, front desk, warehouse leads) | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery |

**Not a fit:** marketing websites, IT support, a developer by the hour, or a problem too small for a $20,000 Pilot. Say so early and kindly, and log the reason in the CRM.

**The four qualifying questions**, all asked before you spend real time on a lead:
1. Why this? What problem, in their words?
2. Why now? What changed that makes it worth fixing this quarter?
3. Why us? What makes WalterSignal the right fit over doing nothing or buying a tool?
4. Is it big enough? Would fixing it be worth more to them than a $20,000 Pilot?

The folder has two client stories, Brennan Mechanical (HVAC) and Harbor Dental Group. They are module 3, on day 2, so you aren't expected to tell them yet. Sorting five sample businesses into fit and not a fit is also day 2.
