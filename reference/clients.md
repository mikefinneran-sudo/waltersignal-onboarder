# Who We Work With

WalterSignal does client work: it builds software for one client at a time, to fit how that
client works. This page says which businesses are a good fit, who you talk to inside them, and
how to tell fit from not-fit.

## A Good-Fit Client

- **Size:** an established business of roughly 10 to 250 employees, with revenue coming in and a
  real operation to improve. Not a startup still finding customers.
- **Situation:** the work runs on spreadsheets, whiteboards, email, and paper, or on several
  tools that do not share information, so people type the same thing twice.
- **Signs you will hear:** "Quotes take us two days." "Only Sarah knows how that works." "We
  re-enter every order into the accounting system." "I can't see where jobs stand without
  calling around."
- **Common industries:** field service (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), distribution and wholesale,
  light manufacturing, multi-location healthcare offices (dental, physical therapy), and
  professional services firms. No industry is excluded; judge each business on its own facts.

## Who You Talk To

| Role at the client | What they do in the deal | How to work with them |
|---|---|---|
| **Decider:** the owner, CEO, or general manager | Signs the agreement and approves the money | Talk about cost, risk, and what they will be able to see and control |
| **Champion:** usually the operations manager or office manager | Lives with the problem every day and knows where work falls through the cracks | Win them first. They tell you how the work really runs, and the decider listens to them |
| **Users:** dispatchers, estimators, front-desk staff, warehouse leads | Will use what gets built | Ask to talk to one during discovery; their answers make the Discovery Sprint sharper |

## How Long Deals Take

- First call to a signed Discovery Sprint: usually 2 to 4 weeks.
- Most clients decide on a Pilot within 30 days of the Sprint readout.
- A Build is decided after the Pilot shows its number (`services.md`).

## Not a Fit

Say so early and kindly, and log the reason in the CRM (`sales-process.md`).

| They want | Why it is not a fit | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| A website or marketing | WalterSignal does not build marketing websites | "That isn't work we do. We build the software that runs the operation behind the website." |
| IT support, computers, or networks | Not client work WalterSignal does | "That's an IT provider's job, not ours." |
| A developer by the hour | WalterSignal sells scoped phases, not hours (`services.md`) | "We work in fixed phases so you know the cost before we start." |
| A problem too small for a $20,000 Pilot | The smallest phase after the Sprint is $20,000 | Log it as Closed Lost with the reason "too small" |

## The Four Qualifying Questions

Ask all four before spending real time on a lead:

1. **Why this?** What problem, in their words?
2. **Why now?** What changed that makes it worth fixing this quarter?
3. **Why us?** What would make WalterSignal the right fit over doing nothing or buying a tool?
4. **Is it big enough?** Would fixing it be worth more to them than a $20,000 Pilot?

If a lead cannot answer "Why now?", log it and set a follow-up date. Do not chase it.
