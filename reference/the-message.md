# The Pitch

This file gives the one message every WalterSignal conversation carries: a 30-second pitch, the
four parts to cover in the first two minutes, and what never to say. On day one you learn it and say it to the Head of Sales only. The pitch rests on the four-part Core Philosophy below. The pitch itself carries one idea: nobody builds anything
until they have seen how your work runs.

## Core Philosophy

Four parts, in this order. Every price, promise, and pitch must agree with all four.

1. **Transparency.** Prices, scope, and what is and is not possible are stated plainly, and there
   are no hidden fees. Work outside the signed agreement is quoted in writing before it starts
   (`what-we-sell.md`, "Changes Outside the Agreement"). If something cannot be done or is not
   known, say so and name the owner (`who-owns-what.md`).
2. **Useful implementations.** WalterSignal builds what fixes a real problem in the client's work,
   proven on the client's own numbers, not technology for its own sake.
3. **Client-owned applications.** The client owns the code, the data, and the accounts.
4. **No mandatory monthly fees.** Support Plans exist but are optional, and the client may take
   none. There are no per-user fees and no license fees (`what-we-sell.md`).

## The 30-Second Pitch

> WalterSignal builds software for established businesses, and you see one fix working on your own
> numbers before you buy the rest. At Brennan Mechanical, a 38-person contractor, every quote took
> two days, so estimators spent their week rebuilding quotes. After the Pilot, quotes went out in four hours. How long
> does a quote take at your company today, and how fast would it need to be?

Say it in your own words once you know it. It carries one idea (see it work first), the Brennan proof, and it ends with the question. The four parts below are covered in the first two minutes of the call.

## The Value Statement

Use this form to check the pitch is complete:

- **For** established businesses of roughly 10 to 250 people
- **who** run on spreadsheets, whiteboards, and tools that do not talk to each other,
- **WalterSignal is** a software and AI consultancy
- **that** maps the work first, proves one fix on the client's own numbers, and then builds the
  rest.
- **Unlike** a software vendor that sells a subscription or a freelancer who builds without
  looking at the work, the client owns everything built: the code, the data, and the accounts.

## The Insight Behind It

Most owners believe they need new software. In most of the businesses WalterSignal meets, the cost is the
same job typed twice and a decision made late because nobody can see the numbers. Say this when a
prospect asks "what do you do?" so the conversation starts with their work, not the technology. This is why WalterSignal maps the work first and proves one fix before it builds the rest.

## The Four Parts to Cover in the First Two Minutes

| Part | What it says | Why it lands with the client | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who it is for** | Established businesses, roughly 10 to 250 people, running on spreadsheets, whiteboards, and tools that do not talk to each other | They recognize themselves, or they are not a fit (`clients.md`) | Name the situation, not the industry |
| **Understand first** | We map how the work runs before recommending anything | They have been pitched tools by people who never asked how they work | Ask how the work runs before you describe anything |
| **Prove before building** | One workflow goes live and is measured before the full build | They never fund a large project on the strength of a slide | Say "on your own numbers" |
| **You own it** | The client owns the code, the data, and the accounts | No lock-in, no per-user or license fees, and no mandatory monthly fee (Support Plans are optional) | Say it again at the end of the call |

## Say This

- "Before we recommend anything, we want to see how the work actually runs."
- "You'll see one piece working on your own numbers before you commit to the rest."
- "You own what we build: the code, the data, and the accounts are in your name."
- "If AI is not the right answer for a step, we will tell you."

## Never Say This

- Never promise a delivery date. Dates come from Solutions Engineering after scoping.
- Never promise a result (hours saved, revenue gained) for a prospect's business. The Brennan Mechanical result belongs to that client and is told as theirs.
- Never discount, or hint at one. Discounts belong to the Founder & CEO.
- Never lead with the technology ("AI agents," "LLMs," tool names). Lead with the client's work.
- Never claim WalterSignal sells a product or a software subscription, and never say a monthly fee is required. Support Plans are optional. It does client work only.
- Never hide or soften a limit, a price rule, or a fee. Transparency is part one of the Core Philosophy.
