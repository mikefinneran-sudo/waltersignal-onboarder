# Templates and Examples

This file holds the four templates you use on every deal: the research brief, the discovery call
guide, the call note, and the follow-up email, plus one insight-led outreach template. Each shows
its purpose, a filled example, and what each placeholder in square brackets means.

Copy these, and save each one in the client's folder in the Vault (`second-brain.md`). Each template is followed by a filled example. The example business is made up for
practice; it is not a client.

## Research Brief (One Page, Before Every Call)

**Purpose:** you never ask a question you could have read. **Ask:** all 8 fields filled and saved in
the Vault before the call.

```
Business:            name, town, what they do, how many people
Fit:                 why it fits (clients.md), or why it may not
Who decides:         the owner-operator's name, if public
Likely champion:     the office manager or equivalent, if public
What they use now:   tools visible on their website or job posts
Why-now signals:     new location, hiring, reviews, a season coming
Three questions:     the first three you will ask, from discovery-questions.md
Sources:             every link you used
```

### Example: Brief

```
Business:            Example Heating & Air (practice only), Springfield, residential HVAC, ~25 people
Fit:                 Good. Growing, hiring, and the job post asks for spreadsheet skills.
Who decides:         The owner, named on the About page
Likely champion:     Office manager, named in a job post for a second dispatcher
What they use now:   Online booking widget on the site; job post asks for "spreadsheet skills"
Why-now signals:     Hiring a second dispatcher; heating season starting
Three questions:     #4 (call to job), #6 (falls through the cracks), #12 (why this quarter)
Sources:             company website, job post, Google Business Profile
```

## Discovery Call Guide (Open on Your Screen During the Call)

**Purpose:** keep the call in the four steps of `sales-process.md`. **Ask:** end with a dated next step.

```
Opening (2 min):     [Name], thanks for the time. You asked about [reason they reached out]. I will ask
                     about how the work runs, then tell you whether and how we could help. Is that fine?
Situation (5 min):   two or three questions from discovery-questions.md, groups Situation
Problem (5 min):     "Where does [the job] fall through the cracks?"
Implication (5 min): "How many times a month, and what does one cost you?"
Need-payoff (5 min): "What number would tell you it is fixed?"
Close (3 min):       "Does [day, date] work for [next step]? I will send the invite today." Owner: you, who sends the invite and logs the step in the CRM the same day.
```

Placeholders: [Name] is the person you are speaking to; [reason] is their words from the first
message; [the job] is the task they named; [day, date] is a real calendar date.

## CRM Stage Rules

| Stage | Enter when | Required fields | Exit when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leads | A prospect is identified | Four qualifying answers, source, owner (you), dated next step | A proposal is sent |
| Proposals | A written proposal is sent | Proposal date, the three options, owner (you, with the Head of Sales approving), dated follow-up | Signed agreement returns, or the prospect says no |
| Closed Won | Signed agreement returned | Signing date, Head of Sales told | Delivery Lead takes over |
| Closed Lost | No, silence after three dated follow-ups, or not a fit | Reason | Prospect returns with a new need |

## Call Note (Same Day, in the Vault; Next Step and Stage in the CRM)

```
Date and attendees:  today's date; every name and title on the call
Current state:       how the work runs today, in their words
Gap:                 what it costs them, with their numbers (hours, missed jobs, errors)
Future state:        the number that would tell them it is fixed, in their words
Cost of the gap:     the difference between today and that number, per month
Why now:              the event that makes this urgent, in their words
Decider / champion:   who signs, and who pushes for it internally
Next step:           DATE, OWNER, WHAT HAPPENS
Stage:               Leads / Proposals / Closed Won / Closed Lost
```

### Example: Call Note

```
Date and attendees:  Tue, owner and office manager (practice only)
Current state:       Calls go to a cell phone; jobs written on a whiteboard; invoices typed weekly
Gap:                 Owner estimates 3 missed callbacks a week in season; office manager re-keys every job
Future state:        No missed callbacks in a week, jobs entered once
Cost of the gap:     About 12 missed callbacks a month, plus about 6 hours a week of re-keying
Why now:             Second dispatcher starts next month; wants one system before then
Decider / champion:  Owner decides; office manager is the champion
Next step:           Fri, you, send Discovery Sprint overview (draft approved first)
Stage:               Leads
```

## Follow-Up Email (Draft for Approval)

```
Subject: Next step from our call

[Name], thank you for walking me through how [the work] runs today.

What I heard: [the gap, in their words].
What I am asking: please confirm [the next step] for [date, with owner].

[Your name]
WalterSignal
```

Purpose of the follow-up: confirm what you heard and the one next step. Owner of the next step: the person named in [date, with owner], usually you. Placeholders: [Name] is the prospect; [the work] is their process in their words; [the gap] is the cost they gave you; [the next step] is the dated step from the call; [date, with owner] is a calendar date and the person who acts.

Every draft goes to the Head of Sales before it is sent.

## Insight-Led Outreach Email (Use From CE5, With Approval)

**Purpose:** open a conversation with a business that fits (`clients.md`) by naming a problem you
noticed in their work, not by describing WalterSignal. **Ask:** one reply by the date you set in the email (owner: you, who calls on the date given).

```
Subject: [Their process] at [Business]

[Name], [business] is hiring a second dispatcher [or other why-now signal from your brief], which
is usually when jobs get re-keyed twice and quotes slow down. Most owners assume the fix is another tool; the slowdown is usually in how the work is handed from one person to the next.

A 38-person contractor we worked with cut the time to send a quote from two days to four hours
after we mapped how the work ran. How do quotes get from request to customer at [Business] today? Please reply by [reply-by date] and I will call you [call date].

[Your name]
WalterSignal
```

Placeholders: [their process] is the work you noticed; [Business] and [Name] come from the brief; [reply-by date] and [call date] are real calendar dates, the first for the prospect and the second for you;
[why-now signal] is from the Why-now field. The Head of Sales approves every outreach email before
it is sent.
