# Case Studies

This file holds two client stories you tell as proof. Both are fictional, written for this
exercise. Tell each as the client's story, with the client's numbers. Never turn a client's result
into a promise for a prospect (`the-message.md`).

Pick the story closest to the prospect's industry or problem. Brennan shows all three phases.
Harbor shows a client who stopped after the Pilot, which is allowed and is part of the pitch. Each
story follows the same order: the challenge, what was done, the outcome.

## Brennan Mechanical: Quotes in Hours, Not Days

**The client.** A 38-person heating, cooling, and plumbing contractor serving three counties.
Owner: the decider. Office manager: the champion.

**Where they started (current state).** Quotes took two business days because an estimator rebuilt
each one from a spreadsheet price book and old jobs. Dispatch ran on a whiteboard. Every finished
job was typed again into the accounting software.

**Where they wanted to be (future state).** A quote out the same day it was requested, measured as
the time from request to quote sent.

**The gap and what it cost.** Two business days per quote, for every quote. The estimators spent
their hours rebuilding quotes instead of pricing new work.

**What was done.**

| Phase | What happened | How long | Price paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery Sprint** | Interviews with the owner, office manager, two estimators, and a dispatcher. Ranked quoting first, dispatch second, and left the accounting software alone. | 2 weeks | $7,500, credited to the Pilot |
| **Pilot** | A quote builder that pulls the price book and past jobs and drafts each quote with an AI step; an estimator approves every quote before it goes out. The agreed number: time from request to quote sent. | 7 weeks | $30,000 (AI step and three connected tools), less the $7,500 credit |
| **Build** | A dispatch board, a technician phone app for job notes and photos, and an automatic hand-off of finished jobs to the accounting software. Staff trained by role. | 5 months | $140,000, in three modules |

**The outcome (Brennan's numbers).** Median time to send a quote fell from two business days to
four hours during the Pilot. After the Build, finished jobs stopped being typed twice. Brennan now
pays for the Standard Support Plan.

**Where the numbers come from.** The quote time is the Pilot measurement taken against the number
agreed at the start. The Build result is Brennan's own account after go-live.

**What to point out.** The Sprint ranked the accounting software last and left it alone. WalterSignal recommends less when less is right.

**Use this story when** the prospect says "AI is all hype" or "do you have results" (`objections.md`),
or when the problem is slow quotes, whiteboard dispatch, or retyping work.

## Harbor Dental Group: A Pilot, and a Pause

**The client.** A four-office dental group with about 60 staff. Operations director: the
champion. The two dentist-owners: the deciders.

**Where they started (current state).** Front-desk staff spent 10 to 15 minutes per patient
calling insurers to check coverage before each appointment.

**Where they wanted to be (future state).** Coverage checked ahead of the appointment, with the
staff minutes per check as the one agreed number.

**The gap and what it cost.** About 12 minutes of front-desk time on every check, repeated for
every patient, across four offices.

**What was done.**

| Phase | What happened | How long | Price paid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery Sprint** | Shadowed the front desk at two offices. Ranked insurance checks first. | 2 weeks | $7,500, credited to the Pilot |
| **Pilot** | Coverage checks run the night before each appointment through the insurers' online portals; an AI step summarizes the coverage and front-desk staff approve each summary. The agreed number: staff minutes per check. | 6 weeks | $20,000 (two connected tools), less the $7,500 credit |

**The outcome (Harbor's numbers).** Staff time per check fell from about 12 minutes to about 3.
Harbor chose not to start a Build yet; it kept the Pilot running on the Essentials Support Plan
and owns everything built so far.

**Where the numbers come from.** Both minute figures are the Pilot measurement of staff minutes per
check, taken against the number agreed at the start.

**What to point out.** Each phase stands on its own. A client can stop after any phase and keep
what was delivered (`what-we-sell.md`).

**Use this story when** the prospect says "what happens if you go away" or "we need to think about
it," or when the problem is staff time spent on repeated checks (`objections.md`).
