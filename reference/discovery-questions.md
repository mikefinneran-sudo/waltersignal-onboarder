# Discovery Question Bank

This file lists the questions to ask on a discovery call, grouped by the four SPIN question types
(Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff), in the order of the call in `sales-process.md`.
Pick two or three per group. A discovery call is a conversation, not a survey. Ask one question at
a time, then stop talking.

## Opening: Why Are We Talking?

1. "What made you reach out now?"
2. "How did you hear about us?" (Log the source in the CRM: referral, website, event, or
   outreach.)

## Situation: How Does the Work Run Today?

Facts about their business. Ask few of these; use your research brief (`templates.md`) so you do
not ask what you can read.

3. "Walk me through what happens from the moment a customer calls you."
4. "Which tools does anyone open every week?"
5. "Who in the office lives with this every day?" (That is usually the champion.)
6. "What gets written on paper, or kept in someone's head?"

## Problem: What Is Not Working?

7. "Where does a job fall through the cracks?"
8. "What do you do when it does?"
9. "Which part of this do your people dislike most?"

## Implication: What Does It Cost?

Make the cost of the problem concrete, in their numbers, before you mention a solution.

10. "How many times a month does that happen?"
11. "What does one of those cost you, in hours or in a lost job?"
12. "What happens to a job when the one person who knows the process is out sick?" (This surfaces a
    risk the owner may not have counted.)
13. "If nothing changes, what does this look like a year from now?"

## Need-Payoff: What Would Fixing It Be Worth?

The buyer says the value; you do not claim it.

14. "If that happened half as often, what would that give you back in a month?"
15. "What would you need to see working to call this fixed?"
16. "What number would tell you it is fixed?" (This is the future state and how they would measure
    it. For a Pilot it becomes the one number the Pilot is measured by (`what-we-sell.md`).)
17. "How big is the difference between today's number and that number, in dollars or hours a
    month?" (This is the size of the gap.)

## Why Now, Why Us

18. "What changed that makes this worth fixing this quarter?"
19. "What have you tried before to fix it?"
20. "What happened when you tried it?"

## Money and Decision

Money comes up by the second conversation at the latest.

21. "Who besides you weighs in on a decision like this?"
22. "Do you have a range in mind, or would it help to see options?"
23. "When would you want this working?"

## Next Step (Every Call)

24. "Does Thursday, December 3, work for a second call with you and your office manager?" Write the next step in the CRM the same day: a date, an owner, and
    what happens.

## Never Ask

- "Do you want to use AI?" Lead with their work, not the technology (`the-message.md`).
- "What's your budget?" in the first five minutes. Earn it with the Implication questions first.
- Anything about competitors' private pricing or the prospect's personal details.
- Two questions in one breath. Ask the first, listen, then ask the second.
