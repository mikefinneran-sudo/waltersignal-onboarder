# Who We Sell To

## Buyer 1: The Advisory Buyer

- Small and lower-mid-market businesses that know AI matters but cannot fund an enterprise
  program to work it out. No industry is excluded; each business is judged on its own facts.
- **Who decides:** the owner-operator. They sign.
- **Who champions:** the office manager. They live with the problem every day and will tell you
  where the work falls through the cracks. Win them and the owner listens.
- **How long it takes:** a decision usually comes in 7 to 14 days. Procurement friction is low.
- **Front door:** the Signal Audit, for buyers who want a plan first (`offers.md`).

## Buyer 2: The Get-Me-Online Buyer

- Small local operators who are barely online: trades, equipment, homestead, and rural service
  businesses. Many arrive from the Dig Drive DIY YouTube channel, on a phone, mid-scroll.
- They want a number up front. Hiding the price reads as a red flag to them, so their page,
  waltersignal.io/dig, shows prices (`offers.md`).
- They are not looking for advice. They want to be found and called.

## Why They Are Never Mixed

The two buyers want different things. Do not pitch the advisory process to someone who wants a
landing page, and do not hand an advisory buyer a price before the conversation. Ask early which
one you are talking to.

## The Three Qualifying Questions

Ask all three before spending real time on a lead:

1. **Why this?** What problem, in their words?
2. **Why now?** What changed that makes it worth solving this quarter?
3. **Why us?** What would make WalterSignal the right fit over doing nothing?

If a lead cannot answer "Why now?", log it and set a follow-up date. Do not chase it.
