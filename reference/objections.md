# Objections

This file teaches the five-step answer to a buyer's objection and gives the words for the nine
objections a new Sales Executive hears most. It is module 6, taught on days 8 to 10
(`syllabus.md`).

## The Five Steps

An objection is a question the buyer has not finished asking. Answer in this order every time.
Do not skip to the answer.

| Step | What you do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Acknowledge | Say back what you heard, in your own words, and agree the worry is reasonable. Do not defend. | A buyer who feels heard stops repeating the objection. |
| 2. Ask | Ask one open question about their situation: what happened before, what they are comparing against, what the worry costs them. | The buyer's own words show what sits underneath. |
| 3. Isolate | Ask whether this is the only thing in the way: "If we settled that, is there anything else?" | A second objection hiding behind the first is found now, not after your answer. |
| 4. Prove | Answer with one named fact from `case-study.md` or `what-we-sell.md`, with its number, told as that client's result. | A specific story is believed. A claim is not. |
| 5. Confirm and Next Step | Ask "Does that answer it?" Then agree one next step with an owner and a calendar date. | A call with no dated next step does not move the deal (`sales-process.md`). |

Rules that hold for every objection:

- Never promise the prospect the number another client got (`the-message.md`).
- Never offer, hint at, or agree to a discount. The Founder & CEO decides every discount.
- Before the Launch Gate on day 10 you quote no price to anyone. Where an answer below contains a
  price, you say it in practice only (`what-we-sell.md`).
- If the question is outside this folder, say you will find out and name the title that owns it
  (`who-owns-what.md`). Never guess.

## How to Practice It

Practice follows I do, we do, you do (`syllabus.md`).

1. **I do (day 8 or 9).** The Head of Sales plays the buyer and answers two objections while you
   watch. You write down which step each sentence belongs to.
2. **We do (day 9).** The Head of Sales plays the buyer on two more. You give the Acknowledge and
   Ask steps; the Head of Sales supplies the rest and corrects you.
3. **You do (day 9).** Five objections, one after another, with no script in front of you. You pass
   an objection if you complete all five steps in order, name a real fact with its number, and
   agree a dated next step.
4. **If you miss.** You repeat only the objections you missed, with new wording from the Head of
   Sales, at the next daily check-in. Module 6 is not signed off until all five pass.

## The Nine Objections

### "AI Is All Hype."

- **Buyer may mean:** they have been sold to and burned. Check it; do not assume it.
- **Acknowledge:** "That is a fair thing to be wary of. A lot of what is sold as AI does not earn
  its price."
- **Ask:** "What did you try, or see someone else try, that did not work out?"
- **Isolate:** "If I can show you where we would not use AI at all, is there anything else that
  would keep you from a first conversation?"
- **Prove:** "Brennan Mechanical, a 38-person contractor, got a Discovery Sprint that told it to
  leave its accounting software alone and fix quoting first. Quotes then went from two business
  days to four hours." (`case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that sound like the straight answer you were missing? I will
  send you a one-page summary of how we work by Thursday, December 3, and you tell me by Monday,
  December 7, whether the owner should join a second call."
- **Weak:** "AI is the future, and we are experts." It claims and proves nothing.
- **Right:** a question about what burned them, then one named story with a number.

### "Just Send Me a Price."

- **Buyer may mean:** they want to know whether this is worth their time.
- **Acknowledge:** "That makes sense. You should not spend an hour before you know the size of
  the commitment."
- **Ask:** "What problem do you want solved, and what is it costing you today?"
- **Isolate:** "If the price fits, is there anything else you would need before a next step?"
- **Prove (before launch):** you quote no price. Say "Let me get you the right person for that,"
  and bring in the Head of Sales.
- **Prove (after launch):** "The first step is a two-week Discovery Sprint at a fixed $7,500. It
  is credited in full toward a Pilot if you sign within 30 days of the readout." Harbor Dental
  Group's Sprint was credited toward its $20,000 Pilot. (`what-we-sell.md`, `case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Is a fixed $7,500 for two weeks a size you can work with? I will
  send the one-page Sprint proposal, approved by my Head of Sales, by Friday, December 4."
- **Never:** guess a Build price or a range. The Founder & CEO prices every Build.
- **Weak:** "Pricing depends on many factors, and we offer great value." It dodges and gives no
  number.
- **Right:** a question about the problem first, then the fixed Sprint price, said only after
  launch.

### "We Already Pay for Software."

- **Buyer may mean:** they fear another monthly bill.
- **Acknowledge:** "A new subscription on top of what you already pay is the last thing you want."
- **Ask:** "Which of the tools you pay for does anyone actually open every week?"
- **Isolate:** "If there were no monthly fee for what we build, would anything else stand in the
  way?"
- **Prove:** "What we build, you own: no license fees and no per-user fees. Brennan Mechanical owns
  everything built for it, and pays $3,000 a month for a Standard Support Plan that is optional
  and month to month." (`what-we-sell.md`, `case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that change how you see the cost? Please send me the list of
  tools you pay for by Wednesday, December 2, and I will bring it to our next call."
- **Weak:** "Our software is better than what you have." It starts a feature argument.
- **Right:** a question about which tools go unopened, then ownership with no per-user fee.

### "What Happens if You Go Away?"

- **Buyer may mean:** a 14-person firm feels risky.
- **Acknowledge:** "A small vendor should be asked that. You are right to want an answer."
- **Ask:** "What would you need to be able to do yourselves if we were gone next month?"
- **Isolate:** "If that were covered, is any other part of the risk bothering you?"
- **Prove:** "The code, the data, and every account are in your name, so another developer can pick
  it up. Harbor Dental Group stopped after its Pilot, kept everything built, and its staff checks
  now take about 3 minutes instead of about 12." (`what-we-sell.md`, `case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that answer the risk? I will ask our Solutions Engineering lead
  to send you what a handover looks like by Thursday, December 3."
- **Weak:** "We are not going anywhere." It is a promise nobody can check.
- **Right:** what the client holds if the vendor disappears, then a client who stopped and kept it.

### "Can You Do It Cheaper?"

- **Buyer may mean:** price anchoring, or a real budget limit.
- **Acknowledge:** "Budget is a real limit, and I will not pretend otherwise."
- **Ask:** "What number were you hoping for, and what is it based on?"
- **Isolate:** "If budget were settled, is anything else holding this back?"
- **Prove:** "We sell three phases, each signed on its own. You can stop after the two-week
  Discovery Sprint and keep the map and the ranked list. Brennan Mechanical's Sprint also found
  what not to build." (`what-we-sell.md`, `case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does a smaller first step help? I will take your budget question to
  our Founder & CEO today and tell you what they say by Friday, December 4."
- **Never:** offer or hint at a discount. Route the question to the Founder & CEO.
- **Weak:** "I can see what I can do on the price." It signals that a discount exists.
- **Right:** a question about the number, then a smaller first phase, then the Founder & CEO.

### "We Need to Think About It."

- **Buyer may mean:** no clear reason to act now, or someone else decides.
- **Acknowledge:** "Of course. A decision like this should not be rushed."
- **Ask:** "What would you want to know before you can decide?"
- **Isolate:** "If I answered that, is it the only thing left, or does someone else need to weigh
  in?"
- **Prove:** "If you sign a Pilot within 30 days of the Sprint readout, the $7,500 is credited in
  full, so waiting has a real cost." (`what-we-sell.md`) This applies after a Sprint, not before.
- **Confirm and next step:** "Who else should be on the next call? Let us book 30 minutes on
  Tuesday, December 8, and I will send the invite today."
- **Weak:** "No problem, I will follow up in a few weeks." The deal now has no date.
- **Right:** a question about what is missing, who else decides, and a booked date.

### "Do You Have Results?"

- **Buyer may mean:** they want proof from a business like theirs.
- **Acknowledge:** "You should see proof, not a slide."
- **Ask:** "What kind of result would convince you, and what does the problem cost you today?"
- **Isolate:** "If you saw that result from a business like yours, would anything else hold you
  back?"
- **Prove:** pick the closer story and tell it as the client's own. Brennan Mechanical: median
  time to send a quote fell from two business days to four hours in the Pilot. Harbor Dental Group:
  staff time per insurance check fell from about 12 minutes to about 3. (`case-study.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that look like your problem? I will send the full story by
  Thursday, December 3, and we can talk about your own number on Monday, December 7."
- **Never:** promise the prospect the same number.
- **Weak:** "Our clients are very happy." It has no name and no number.
- **Right:** one named client, the number they measured, told as theirs.

### "Is Our Data Safe? Where Does It Run?"

- **Buyer may mean:** a real risk question. Treat it as one.
- **Acknowledge:** "That is the right question to ask before anything is built."
- **Ask:** "What data worries you most, and does a rule or a contract govern it?"
- **Isolate:** "If you had a straight answer on that, is there anything else on security?"
- **Prove:** this folder does not answer security questions, so you do not. The one fact you can
  state is that every account is in the client's name (`what-we-sell.md`). Say "I will get you a
  straight answer from our Solutions Engineering lead." (`who-owns-what.md`)
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that sound fair? I will send your two questions to Solutions
  Engineering today, and the answer comes to you by Friday, December 4."
- **Weak:** "Do not worry, it is all very secure." It invents a claim.
- **Right:** ask what data worries them, then route it to Solutions Engineering.

### "How Long Will It Take?"

- **Buyer may mean:** they have a date that matters, such as a busy season.
- **Acknowledge:** "A date matters when the work depends on a season."
- **Ask:** "What date are you working to, and what happens if it slips?"
- **Isolate:** "Is that the only hard date, or is there another one?"
- **Prove:** "A Discovery Sprint takes 2 weeks and a Pilot 6 to 8 weeks. Brennan Mechanical's Pilot
  took 7 weeks and its Build 5 months. A Build is usually 3 to 6 months." (`what-we-sell.md`,
  `case-study.md`) You never promise a date; Solutions Engineering sets dates after scoping.
- **Confirm and next step:** "Does that fit your date? I will ask Solutions Engineering what is
  realistic and tell you by Friday, December 4."
- **Weak:** "We can have it done before your busy season." It promises a date.
- **Right:** ask the date and its cost, give the phase lengths, route the date to Solutions
  Engineering.

## An Objection That Is Not Listed

Use the same five steps. Take the Prove step only from `case-study.md` or `what-we-sell.md`. If
no fact there answers it, say "I will find out" and name the owning title (`who-owns-what.md`).
Add the objection to `question-log.md`. Never invent proof.
